Rental strategy

Short-term, seasonal or mixed: what works best in Cervinia?

For most apartments in Cervinia, short-term rental with flexible dates works better than the classic Saturday-to-Saturday week. Guests coming to Cervinia today look for 3–5 night stays, and a rigid calendar leaves exactly the most valuable days empty.

We see it in the apartments we manage: weekly rentals are in low demand, and Cervinia visitors want flexible dates, often for 3 or 5 nights. That is why we set minimum stays by period instead of seven-day blocks.

A seasonal rental, meaning one tenant for the whole winter, is still an option for owners who want less turnover and accept lower income. The choice depends on location, distance from the lifts, number of beds, parking and how much you want to use the home yourself: we decide it together during the assessment.

Regulations and compliance

What paperwork do you need to rent out a home in Cervinia in 2026?

To rent an apartment in Cervinia to tourists you need the CIR issued by the municipality and the national CIN code, guest registration with the police, ISTAT statistical reporting, and collection of the tourist tax. We handle all of it, from requesting the codes to the filings for every stay.

CIR (regional code)Issued by the municipality after the owner's declaration, and shown clearly in every listing (Aosta Valley Regional Law 11/2023)Vesta, registered to Vesta

Unlike many managers, we register the property's CIR and CIN in Vesta's name. That makes us responsible for every filing: if a requirement were missed or done wrong, any administrative fine would fall on Vesta, not on you, and you would never have to pay it first and then claim it back from us.

The CIN must also appear in short-term rental listings, alongside the regional code. For rules that apply across the region, see our page on short-term rental management in the Aosta Valley.

Tax management

What tax advantages do we handle for you?

Under the cedolare secca flat-tax regime, short-term rental income is taxed at 26%, reduced to 21% if you rent out only one property in the year. As the intermediary we act as withholding agent: we apply the withholding required by law and pay it ourselves, so you do not have to do the maths for every booking.

The rules are set out in the Italian Revenue Agency's guide to short-term rentals (April 2026, in Italian). Two points to keep in mind:

  • the withholding: as withholding agent we apply and pay the withholding required by law;
  • the two-apartment limit: from 2026 the short-term rental regime only applies if you use no more than two apartments for this purpose in a year; above that, the activity is presumed to be a business.

We help you choose the most favourable option and, where allowed, treat part of the expenses as reimbursements so they are not counted as income.
