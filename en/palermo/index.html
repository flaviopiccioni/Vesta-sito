Why Palermo

What is the short-term rental market like in Palermo?

Palermo is visited all year round, and increasingly from abroad: in 2025 foreign visitors accounted for 63.8% of overnight stays in the city, up from 54.7% in 2019, according to the Sicilian Region's tourism observatory, in figures released in March 2026. Official overnight stays were around 2.1 million, 7.3% more than in 2024, and they exceed 3.1 million once private rental homes are included.

Seasonality also weighs less than elsewhere: the Sicilian Region places the province of Palermo among those with the mildest seasonality on the island. For an owner, that means a calendar to work every month and international guests to look after in several languages, from the first enquiry to departure.

Demand is shared across many listings, though: in 2024 the City of Palermo had 10,493 active Airbnb homes, up from 6,896 in 2017, according to the AirMap study by the Politecnico di Torino. Over the same period, average booked nights per listing rose from about 45 to about 95 a year: guests choose on photos, price and reviews.

Palermo is one of the two locations where we offer short-term rental management in Sicily, together with Marzamemi, where we already manage holiday homes.

Where we operate

Which areas of Palermo do you cover for short-term rental management?

We cover the whole city and the coast: the historic centre with its four quarters, Politeama and Libertà, and the seaside at Mondello, Addaura and Sferracavallo. For each area we write the listing and set prices with its guests in mind: those exploring the city on foot, those coming for work, those looking for the sea.

The historic centre and the four quarters

Kalsa, Vucciria, Capo and Ballarò, around the Cassaro, the Quattro Canti, Via Maqueda and Via Roma, are the areas for guests who want to see the city on foot. This is where most of the monuments of Arab-Norman Palermo, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2015 stand, from the Royal Palace with the Palatine Chapel to the Cathedral, as well as the Martorana and San Cataldo. For a home in the centre, walking distance to the sights, quiet evenings and, in a historic palazzo, a well-kept entrance and staircase all matter; the CIN plaque here follows a specific city rule, explained further down.

Politeama, Libertà and La Cala

Between the Teatro Massimo, the Politeama, Via Libertà and the small harbour of La Cala, the city is more orderly and convenient for those arriving for work, for an event or with family. Spacious, bright apartments work well here, including for stays of a few weeks.

Mondello, Addaura and Sferracavallo

On the coast, demand follows the sea more closely, and a home with a terrace or outdoor space becomes the highlight of the listing. The calendar needs a different approach from the centre, with more attention to summer weeks and spring weekends.

Mid-term

Do mid-term rentals make sense in Palermo?

They can, especially for apartments near the university or the hospitals, which work all year round. We manage contracts from 1 to 18 months for out-of-town students, healthcare staff and professionals on assignment, and decide on the data whether to alternate them with short-term rentals.

Demand comes from institutions that never stop: the University of Palermo, with its historic seat in Piazza Marina and the Viale delle Scienze campus, and the city's major hospitals, from the Policlinico to the Ospedale Civico and Villa Sofia-Cervello. Mid-term rentals mean fewer changeovers and steadier income; in exchange, the nightly rate is lower than tourist peaks. That's why we don't choose the model in advance: we compare both scenarios for your home and your area, and the best result is often a mix.

Pricing and seasonality

When is rental demand highest in Palermo?

There is demand in every season: it grows from spring to the August peak and slows in January and February. In 2024, according to the AirMap study of the City of Palermo, listings were booked on average 58% of nights in August and 20% in February, against an annual average of 40%.

September stays high, while November and December fall back close to the winter lows. That's why in Palermo the price isn't set once a season: with a dynamic pricing strategy we update it every day based on events, long weekends and booking pace, with a dedicated revenue management team. In the quieter months we work on minimum stays and longer bookings, including mid-term, so the calendar doesn't sit empty.

Compliance

What paperwork do you need in Palermo, from the Sicilian CIR to the CIN?

First you need the CIR, the regional identification code for Sicily obtained through the portal of the Sicilian Region's Tourism Observatory, then the national CIN: both must appear in every listing, and the CIN must also be displayed outside the building. On top of these come the daily data submissions, guest registration with the police, safety devices and the city tourist tax.

Local rules

What local rules does Palermo have for short-term rentals?

On top of the regional rules, in Palermo the CIN plaque has a required material in the historic centre, and the tourist tax is collected and paid by the holder of the short-term rental. These are the two local rules that most often surprise first-time owners.

In the historic centre, and on listed buildings outside it, the plaque must be in satin-finish burnished brass, rectangular, 20 by 10 centimetres, with the name of the home and the CIN engraved; on buildings of particular value the position must be agreed with the Soprintendenza (heritage authority). Outside the historic centre, plastic or glass is enough, in the same format. These are the instructions the city's tourism department gave managers in January 2025, in agreement with the Soprintendenza, as reported in the press release on the new plaque rules (in Italian).

The tourist tax applies to guests who are not registered residents of Palermo, for up to four consecutive nights. The holder of the short-term rental collects it, issues a receipt and pays it to the city within fifteen days of the end of each quarter, as stated by the City of Palermo tax office (in Italian): with us, Vesta does it.

2026 changes

What changes in 2026 for owners renting in Palermo?

The thresholds have dropped to two homes, both for national tax and under the Sicilian rule. From 2026 the short-term rental regime applies to no more than two apartments, and beyond that a business activity is presumed, as explained by the Italian Revenue Agency (in Italian), and in Sicily tourist letting remains non-business only up to two properties.

For those who already have a CIR, the new regional rules also set a deadline to complete the technical documentation: you'll find the decree and the deadlines on the Sicily page. If this applies to you, we start here in the assessment and check what's missing before publishing any listing.

Tax management

What tax advantages do you get by letting us manage your home?

We act as withholding agent, so we pay the taxes on your rental income and you don't have to chase deadlines. We calculate the flat-rate tax (cedolare secca) on the share that stays with you, net of commission and costs, not on the amount paid by guests.

Where the rules allow it, we classify part of the expenses as expense reimbursements: that way they stay out of revenue and are not taxed. For choices that depend on your personal situation, your accountant remains the reference, and receives tidy figures from us.
