Why Lake Como

Who books a home on Lake Como?

Most guests on the lake come from abroad: in 2024, 86.3% of overnight stays in the province of Como were by foreign visitors, the highest share in Lombardy according to the Lombardy Region 2025 tourism report. For an owner, that means listings, messages and check-in designed for people travelling from abroad, often booking months ahead.

The same report counts 8,029 non-hotel accommodation businesses in the province of Como against 248 hotel businesses: homes and flats make up most of the supply, so competition between listings is strong. Demand covers much of the year, peaking in spring and summer, with wedding dates worth securing early.

Lake Como is one of the places we cover in Lombardy, together with Milan.

Areas we cover

Which areas of Lake Como do you cover?

We look after homes all around the lake, on both the Como and Lecco shores: Como, Cernobbio, Tremezzina, Menaggio and Bellagio in the province of Como; Varenna, Colico and Lecco in the province of Lecco, plus nearby villages. Each shore has its own kind of guest, and the listing tells a different story for each.

Como and Cernobbio

Short-term rental management in Como is aimed at guests who want the town, the historic centre on foot and the ferry on the doorstep, and at people in town on business. What matters here is the position relative to the lakefront and the station, parking and an easy check-in even late in the evening.

Bellagio and the central lake

Bellagio is where we already have homes under management, and we know it well. The central lake, between Bellagio, Tremezzina, Menaggio and Varenna, draws couples, families and wedding guests: here the view, the walk to the ferry pier and dates booked well in advance matter more than elsewhere.

Lecco and the eastern shore

Short-term rental management in Lecco follows the same Lombardy rules as the Como shore, with a more varied audience: lake visitors, those heading into the mountains of the Grigne or the Valsassina, people in town for work. Colico and the upper lake also appeal to guests looking for water sports and active holidays.

Seasonality

Should I rent out all year round or only in high season?

It depends on the home and on how much you want to use it yourself: we decide together, based on data for the area and the calendar you need. Spring and summer bring the highest rates; the quieter months can still fill up with the right prices and minimum stays.

A home in Como or Lecco, handy for business travellers too, can behave differently in the colder months from a villa in a smaller village: we check the data before deciding. If you prefer to rent it out only during the season, we open the calendar for those months and close it when you say so.

Regulations and compliance

What paperwork do you need to rent out a home on Lake Como?

You need the regional CIR code, obtained through a notice to the municipality's SUAP office, and the national CIN code; then guest registration with the police, monthly ISTAT reports on ROSS1000 and the tourist tax, where the municipality applies it. The rules are the same on the Como and Lecco shores, and we handle all of it with the codes registered to Vesta.

2026 changes

What changes for short-term rentals in 2026?

The main change is a cap: from 2026 the short-term rental regime applies to no more than two flats per tax year, and above that threshold the activity is presumed to be a business, as explained by FiscoOggi, the Italian Revenue Agency's magazine, citing the 2026 budget law. The cedolare secca rates stay as they were.

If you have more than two short-term rental homes in total, on the lake or elsewhere, we discuss it during the assessment so you can go to your accountant with a clear picture. Everything else, from CIR and CIN to guest registration, stays the same, and we still handle it.

Tax management

What tax advantages does our management bring you?

We act as withholding agent: we withhold and pay the taxes on every booking, so at year end your figures are already in order for your tax return. We calculate the cedolare secca on your net share, meaning what is left after our fee and costs, not on what guests pay.

Where the rules allow, we classify part of the expenses as expense reimbursements, which stay outside the income you pay tax on. For choices that depend on your personal situation, your accountant remains your point of reference.
